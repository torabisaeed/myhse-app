Calculation Audit — Precision and Mathematical Verification

This section documents the results of a full mathematical audit of the calculation engine. Read it before operating the tool to understand what the numbers mean, how precise they are, and what the known limitations are.

Key Identity: Sp = SRS (always)

Sp = 1 − RHC ÷ PHC = 1 − (1 − SRS) = SRS
Safety Performance is mathematically identical to the Safety Reliability Score — this is a provable identity, not a coincidence.

This identity holds for every hazard column on every assessment. It means the Sp and SRS rows in the table will always show the same number in different formats (fraction vs percentage). If they appear to differ, it is a display rounding artefact only — the underlying calculation is correct.

PHC is a Fixed Anchor

PHC = RHC₁ ÷ (1 − SRS₁)
Computed once from the current condition. Does not change when future barriers are added or modified.

PHC represents the theoretical hazard if all barriers were removed. It is anchored to the current-condition SRS and serves as the invariant denominator for all safety performance calculations. Adding future barriers improves Sp₂ (via SRS₂) but never changes PHC.

Precision and Sources of Uncertainty

SourcePractical impact and mitigation
Arithmetic precisionIEEE 754 double precision (15 significant figures) throughout. Display rounding (3dp HazPoC, 1dp %) never affects intermediate calculations.
Barrier r-valuesEngineering estimates, not measured probabilities. Use the condition multiplier honestly. A barrier in poor condition should use "Degraded" (×0.5), not "As designed" (×1.0).
SRS independence assumptionReal barriers often share failure modes. The SRS may be 5–20% optimistic when barriers are not truly independent. The independence disclaimer in the tool is a mandatory reminder.
Frequency estimationF derived from small samples carries high statistical uncertainty. Use a minimum 3-year data period. Flag low-count categories.
F_ALARP (policy input)SpALARP depends entirely on the F_ALARP/F ratio. Never change F_ALARP without Safety Manager approval and documentation.
Overall model uncertaintyEstimated at ±5% on Sp. Values within 5 percentage points of any threshold require professional judgement and peer review.

Known Edge Cases

🔍 Edge case — F = 0 If no incidents or near misses are recorded for a hazard category, RHC₁ = 0 and PHC = 0, making Sp undefined (0÷0). The tool correctly displays "—" for all derived values. Zero observed events cannot produce a meaningful PHC anchor. Use a minimum F derived from exposure data or FMEA — not purely from observation.
🔍 Edge case — F_ALARP > F (observed frequency) If the target frequency is set above the observed frequency, SpALARP will fall below the current Sp and ALARP will appear demonstrated without any barriers. This is mathematically correct but operationally misleading. Verify F_ALARP is appropriate before accepting a green status in this situation.
🔍 Edge case — High SRS (> 0.99) PHC becomes very sensitive to small r-value changes when SRS is near 0.999. A change of ±0.01 in a single r can move Sp by several percentage points. The high-SRS warning banner activates automatically. Always conduct a sensitivity check (±0.05 on the highest r-values) when this warning appears.

Complete Formula Chain

F = (Incidents + Near Misses) ÷ Data years
Annual event frequency — derived from observed data
T = svcOW × revHrs × 2 × minExp ÷ 60   [collision hazards]
Threat time in hours — both directions of LRV travel
T = trkPct × darkHrs   [track intrusion hazards]
Threat time for track intrusion categories
RHC = S × P × F × E × T ÷ 8
Residual Hazard Cloud per 8-hour shift, in HazPoC units
SRS = 1 − ∏(1 − rᵢ)   [capped at 0.999]
Safety Reliability Score — combined probability at least one barrier intercepts
PHC = RHC₁ ÷ (1 − SRS₁)   [fixed anchor]
Potential Hazard Cloud — theoretical uncontrolled hazard level
AHC = S × P × F_ALARP × E × T ÷ 8
Acceptable Hazard Cloud — tolerable threshold at ALARP target frequency
Sp = 1 − RHC ÷ PHC = SRS   [identity]
Safety Performance — fraction of potential hazard controlled (%)
RHC₂ = PHC × (1 − SRS₂)
Future residual hazard after proposed improvements
Gap closure = (Sp₂ − Sp₁) ÷ (SpALARP − Sp₁)
Fraction of required Sp improvement delivered by future controls

Step-by-Step Assessment Procedure (SOP)

Follow these steps in sequence for every formal crossing assessment. Each step refers to the specific interface element to interact with.

Step 1 — Prepare Before Opening the Tool

Gather the following before opening the Calculator:

  • Incident extract: Confirmed incidents from the safety database, last 3–5 years, for all user groups at this crossing.
  • Near-miss records: Operator reports and CCTV review logs. Do not estimate from memory — retrieve actual records.
  • Track intrusion count: Vehicle encroachments into the track zone from detector logs or CCTV.
  • Service schedule: Current timetable — trains per hour (one way) and daily revenue hours.
  • Previous assessment: If one exists, note prior barrier r-values and ALARP status for continuity.
  • Document reference: Assign a reference number from the safety register before starting.
⚠ Warning Do not estimate near-miss counts from memory. Under-reporting near misses directly lowers F and makes the crossing appear safer than it is.

Step 2 — Open and Configure

Open the tool. Double-click hcm_calculator_v11.html. Confirm "v11" appears in the top bar.
Enter crossing name. Type in the "Crossing / Location" field. This populates all report headers.
Fill the Report Info Bar. Analyst name, agency/organisation, reference number, date, and ALARP margin (default 10% unless otherwise specified by the Safety Manager).
ℹ Note The ALARP margin sets the amber zone width. A 10% margin means a crossing within 10 Sp percentage points of SpALARP is amber, not red. Confirm the approved value before changing.

Step 3 — Enter Incident and Observation Data (Section 1)

Enter every count from your source records. Use 0 for categories with no events — never leave blank.

FieldSourceCommon error
pInc, pNMSafety database + operator NM reportsOmitting near misses — include all close calls
vInc, vNMSafety database + police reportsIncluding events from other crossings in the extract
bInc, bNMSafety database + CCTV logsOften under-reported; use zero only if CCTV confirms none
vIntrDetector logs or CCTV zone entriesCounting gate activations instead of actual zone entries
piInc, piNMObserver reports + CCTVConflating with ped incidents — separate categories
dataYrsStart and end dates of extractAssuming 3 years when the extract covers a different period

T-parameters (set from the current service schedule):

  • svcOW: LRVs per hour, one direction. Use peak-period frequency.
  • revHrs: Total hours per day the line is in service.
  • minExp: Average minutes a road user spends in the hazard zone (default 1 min).
  • trkPct: Fraction of road traffic that enters the track zone — measure from CCTV.
  • darkHrs: Hours per day with reduced visibility. Used only for track intrusion T.
✔ Tip After entering T-parameters, check the calculated F and T in the green cells. F_ped should equal (pInc + pNM) ÷ dataYrs. T_col should equal svcOW × revHrs × 2 × minExp ÷ 60.

Step 4 — Review Hazard Parameters (S, P, E, F_ALARP)

ParamDefaultWhen to change
S (Severity)9 / 6 / 9 / 5 / 8Only if a formal consequence study for this crossing justifies a different rating. Document the basis.
P (Escalation probability)0.8 / 0.5 / 0.9 / 0.3 / 0.8Site-specific data (sight-line study, speed data) can justify adjustment. Peer review required.
E (Exposed persons)1 / 2 / 1 / 2 / 1Adjust for vehicle occupancy data. E=2 for vehicles assumes average occupancy of 2.
F_ALARP0.1 / 0.5 / 0.1 / 1.0 / 0.1 /yrAgency policy input. Do not change without Safety Manager sign-off.
⚠ Warning If F_ALARP is set above the observed F for any category, SpALARP will fall below current Sp and ALARP appears demonstrated without barriers. Verify F_ALARP is appropriate before accepting any green status.

Step 5 — Enter Current Barrier Reliabilities

For each barrier row, enter the r-value for each hazard column where it provides protection. Enter 0 where it does not apply.

Choosing the right r-value

ConditionMultiplierWhen to use
As designed×1.0New installation, recently commissioned to specification.
Good condition×0.9In service, within maintenance schedule, no defects.
Moderate×0.75Some wear, minor maintenance backlog, functionally intact.
Degraded×0.5Visible wear, overdue maintenance, reduced effectiveness.
Poor×0.3Non-compliant with design spec, pending replacement.

For every non-zero r-value, click 📝 and describe what this barrier physically is at this location. These descriptions appear in the Management Report.

Bike column — auto vs manual

By default: SRS_bike = MIN(SRS_Ped, SRS_Veh) × 0.9 for all barriers. To enter site-specific bike r-values for individual barriers, click manual ✎ in that barrier's Bike cell. The SRS combines manual entries with the auto-derived base from remaining auto barriers. Current and future sides are independent.

Step 6 — Check Current ALARP Status

Look at the ALARP Status row, Current Condition side:

  • Green — Record in safety register. Proceed to Step 7.
  • Amber — Record with monitoring note. Increase inspection schedule. Continue to Step 7.
  • Red — Escalate to Safety Manager before proceeding. Do not file the report without a corrective action commitment.

Step 7 — Add Proposed Future Barriers

Three methods — all set current r = 0 by default and must be filled manually:

Method A — Column ＋ button

Click ＋ in the column header for the hazard you are improving.
Select the condition from the dropdown — auto-checks the checkbox.
Review the Final r (= rNom × multiplier, capped at rMax).
Click "Add selected". Enter the current-side r for each added barrier.

Method B — Safety Barrier Library panel

Scroll to the Library panel below the main table. Filter by layer and/or hazard type.
Set the condition dropdown for each barrier — auto-selects.
Click "＋ Add selected to assessment". Enter current r values for each.

Method C — Manual row

Click "+ Add new future safety barrier row". Click ✏ to name it.
Enter r-values directly for both current and future sides. Add 📝 descriptions.

Step 8 — Validate Future Condition

SRS₂ > SRS₁ for each improved column. A barrier with r > 0 should increase SRS.
Gap closure ≥ 50% for any currently red column. Below 50% means proposed improvements are insufficient.
If the High SRS warning banner appears, document a sensitivity check (vary key r by ±0.05 and note the Sp range).
Note any columns still red after improvements — these require corrective action items.

Step 9 — Generate, Review, and Save the Report

Click "📄 Generate Management Report" in the Report Info Bar.
Read the narrative sections — verify barrier names and descriptions are accurate and specific.
Check recommended actions: L1 (foundational) must be addressed before L2 (detection), before L4 (human). If the order seems wrong, check layer badges on barrier rows.
Click "Print / Save PDF". Select A4 Landscape. Save immediately.
Name the file: SAF-YYYY-NNN_[CrossingName]_Assessment.pdf and file in the designated folder.
⚠ Warning Save the PDF before closing the report window. The tool does not auto-save reports. Once the modal is closed, the generated content is gone (inputs remain in local storage).

Step 10 — Approval and Filing

Submit the PDF to the Safety Manager for review and countersignature.
Record reference number, date, current and future ALARP status, and reassessment date in the safety register.
Open a Corrective Action item for each red-status column (current or future).
Schedule the next assessment at the recommended reassessment date shown in the report header.

Worked Example — Pedestrian Collision Hazard

This traces the complete calculation for the Train vs Pedestrian hazard using the default crossing data.

Inputs

ParameterValue
Incidents (pInc) + Near misses (pNM)2 + 11 = 13 events over 3 years
Service frequency (svcOW), revenue hours, exposure6 trains/hr, 15 hrs/day, 1 min exposure
S, P, E, F_ALARP9, 0.8, 1 person, 0.1 events/yr
Current barriersOperator control r=0.5, Ped signal r=0.7
Proposed future additionsTraffic Signal (ped) r=0.6, Road Marker r=0.25, Track Night Visibility r=0.4

Calculation Steps

F = (2 + 11) ÷ 3 = 4.333 events/yr
T = 6 × 15 × 2 × 1 ÷ 60 = 3.000 hrs/day
RHC₁ = 9 × 0.8 × 4.333 × 1 × 3.000 ÷ 8 = 11.700 HazPoC/shift
SRS₁ = 1 − (1−0.5) × (1−0.7) = 1 − 0.15 = 0.850 (85.0%)
PHC = 11.700 ÷ (1 − 0.850) = 11.700 ÷ 0.150 = 78.000 HazPoC/shift [FIXED]
AHC = 9 × 0.8 × 0.1 × 1 × 3.000 ÷ 8 = 0.270 HazPoC/shift
Sp₁ = 1 − 11.700 ÷ 78.000 = 0.850 (85.0%) = SRS₁ ✓
SpALARP = 1 − 0.270 ÷ 78.000 = 99.7% → Gap = 14.7% → Needs enhancement
SRS₂ = 1 − (1−0.5)(1−0.7)(1−0.6)(1−0.25)(1−0.4) = 1 − 0.027 = 0.973 (97.3%)
RHC₂ = 78.000 × (1 − 0.973) = 2.106 HazPoC/shift
Sp₂ = 97.3% → Gap = 2.4% → Within 10% margin → Margin band — monitor
Gap closure = (97.3% − 85.0%) ÷ (99.7% − 85.0%) = 12.3% ÷ 14.7% = 83.9%
✔ Interpretation The proposed future barriers close 83.9% of the required gap. The status improves from red (Needs enhancement) to amber (Margin band — monitor). A 2-year reassessment is recommended.

Safety Barrier Library — Selection and Condition Guide

Engineering Layer Hierarchy

LayerNameRoleExamples
L1Foundational / SystemStructural controls in system design. Highest Sp gain per investment.ATP, grade separation, signal interlock
L2Detection + Active ResponseSensors and systems that detect and respond to developing hazards.Obstacle detection, radar, interlocked gates, CCTV with operator
L3PhysicalPassive barriers preventing or deterring access.Fences, bollards, anti-trespass panels
L4Human + AwarenessControls dependent on human behaviour and compliance.Training, signs, markings, speed restrictions
⚠ Hierarchy Rule Recommended actions always follow L1 → L2 → L3 → L4. Do not skip to L4 improvements before addressing missing L1/L2 controls. L4 alone cannot compensate for a missing foundational layer.

Library r-values

  • rMin: Minimum achievable — barrier in poor condition.
  • rNom: Nominal design reliability — as designed and maintained.
  • rMax: Upper bound — never exceed regardless of condition.

Final r = min(rNom × condition multiplier, rMax) — computed automatically by the tool.

ℹ Note Never assign r = 1.0. No barrier is perfectly reliable. rMax in the library is capped below 1.0 for every entry.

Current vs Future r — The Most Common Mistake

When adding from the library: future r is set automatically (proposed reliability). Current r starts at 0 — you must enter what the barrier's actual reliability is today. Leaving current r at 0 correctly treats the barrier as not yet installed — do not enter a non-zero current r unless the barrier is physically installed and operational.

Deactivate vs Remove

ActionWhen to use
⏸ DeactivateBarrier installed but currently non-functional (e.g., out of service for maintenance). Excluded from SRS. Row kept as audit trail.
✕ RemoveBarrier physically removed from the crossing and will not be reinstalled. Permanently deletes row and frees library entry.

Quality Assurance Checklist

Complete before generating the final report. Every item must be checked by the primary analyst and independently verified by the peer reviewer.

Data Completeness

  • ☐ Incident data covers the full stated period. Dates verified against the source database extract.
  • ☐ Near-miss records retrieved from operator reports AND CCTV logs — not estimated.
  • ☐ Track intrusion count sourced from detector logs or CCTV — not estimated.
  • ☐ Data period (dataYrs) matches the actual extract date range.
  • ☐ T-parameters match the current timetable. svcOW verified against the service schedule.

Parameter Correctness

  • ☐ S, P, E values are at default OR have documented justification.
  • ☐ F_ALARP unchanged from agency policy OR has Safety Manager written approval.
  • ☐ No barrier has r = 1.0 in any column.
  • ☐ Library barriers use realistic condition (not "As designed" for barriers in service >1 year).
  • ☐ Deactivated barriers have a 📝 note explaining why they are excluded.

Results Validation

  • ☐ F values in green cells match (Incidents + NM) ÷ dataYrs.
  • ☐ T_col = svcOW × revHrs × 2 × minExp ÷ 60 (verify displayed value).
  • ☐ High SRS warning banner NOT visible, OR a sensitivity check has been documented.
  • ☐ Gap closure ≥ 50% for all currently red columns, OR a corrective action explains why not.

Report and Filing

  • ☐ PDF saved: SAF-YYYY-NNN_[CrossingName]_Assessment.pdf
  • ☐ Reference number recorded in the safety register.
  • ☐ Corrective Actions opened for all red-status columns.
  • ☐ Next assessment scheduled at the recommended reassessment date.
  • ☐ Peer reviewer has signed off.

Glossary

TermDefinition
AHCAcceptable Hazard Cloud. Tolerable hazard level per shift at the ALARP target frequency: S × P × F_ALARP × E × T ÷ 8.
ALARPAs Low As Reasonably Practicable. Risks must be reduced until further reduction costs are grossly disproportionate to the benefit.
EExposed persons per event.
FAnnual event frequency = (Incidents + Near Misses) ÷ data years.
F_ALARPALARP-acceptable annual frequency. Policy input — not derived from observation.
Gap closure rate(Sp₂ − Sp₁) ÷ (SpALARP − Sp₁). Fraction of required Sp improvement delivered by proposed future controls.
HazPoCHazard Point of Consequence. Unit of hazard: one unit = one risk-weighted hazard exposure per 8-hour shift.
HCMHazard Cloud Model. The proprietary calculation methodology of Dr. Mohammad Saeed Torabi.
PConditional probability of escalation to harm, given a barrier fails.
PHCPotential Hazard Cloud. Theoretical hazard if no barriers existed: RHC₁ ÷ (1 − SRS₁). Fixed anchor.
rReliability value [0, 1]: probability that a barrier prevents escalation in a single exposure event.
RHCResidual Hazard Cloud. Hazard remaining after all active barriers: S × P × F × E × T ÷ 8.
RHC₂Future Residual Hazard Cloud: PHC × (1 − SRS₂).
SSeverity. Consequence magnitude [0–10] if hazard escalates to an accident.
SpSafety Performance = 1 − RHC ÷ PHC = SRS. Fraction of potential hazard controlled (%).
SpALARPMinimum Sp to demonstrate ALARP: 1 − AHC ÷ PHC.
SRSSafety Reliability Score = 1 − ∏(1 − rᵢ), capped at 0.999.
TThreat time. Exposure window: svcOW × revHrs × 2 × minExp ÷ 60 (collision), trkPct × darkHrs (intrusion).
UCHCUncontrolled HazPoC Above AHC = max(RHC − AHC, 0).
